Personal Reflection: Career Strategy and Continuous Learning

What Surprised Me Most
What surprised me the most during this research is the fact that even highly experienced, senior software engineers struggle with the speed of technological change. It made me realize that entering this industry means accepting that I will never truly "finish" studying; graduation is just the beginning of a lifelong learning cycle.

My Concerns and Opportunities
My biggest concern as a university student is finding the right balance. It is easy to feel overwhelmed by the endless list of new frameworks, languages, and tools appearing every month. I often worry that while I am spending months mastering a language like C or studying calculus, the industry might shift toward something completely different, making me feel behind before I even start my career.

However, the opportunity here is massive. If the industry prioritizes practical portfolios and open-source contributions over just paper certificates, it means I have total control over my career path. By building clean personal projects on GitHub and actively contributing to codebases, I can prove my worth directly to global companies, regardless of my age or entry-level status.

How I Will Adapt Personally
To adapt successfully, I will apply the 70/30 rule we discovered. I will not chase every single new tech trend. Instead, I will focus 70% of my energy on mastering the hardcore foundations—data structures, algorithms, and software design principles—because those never change. I will use the remaining 30% to experiment with new tools and AI coding assistants to speed up my workflow. Most importantly, I will commit to keeping my GitHub active, ensuring that everything I learn practically is documented publicly through clear, well-structured repositories.
